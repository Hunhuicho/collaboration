01

The case

One order line, one requested date — and a confirmation that comes back in two pieces.

PO issued 100 EA Item 00010 RDD 2026-03-10 Status Open
→
Supplier confirmation · schedule 1 50 EA Confirmed date 2026-03-10
Supplier confirmation · schedule 2 50 EA Confirmed date 2026-03-24
The requested date covered all 100. Here the supplier decides on its own to break the quantity apart and move half of it two weeks out. The requester agreed to one delivery and now there are two, with nobody on our side having approved the change.

Then we decide: follow it, or improve on it

With those answers in hand — does MERP simply do what Ariba does, or do we hold a stricter rule of our own? The rest of this document is our starting position for that conversation.

What a "yes" costs us

If Ariba allows the split and we accept it, MERP has to receive and store schedule-level data over EDI — schedule quantity and date per PO line, not just a confirmation flag. That reaches the PO tables, the receipt matching and the screens. It is development, not a configuration switch.

Our starting position

What we would like the rule to be — to be revisited once Ariba has answered.

Provisional

THE ARGUMENTNothing is lost by refusing the split

Purchasing and the supplier agree the plan between themselves. The supplier confirms all 100, ships 50 with a delivery note, and ships the other 50 later. The PO moves to Partially Delivered, then Fully Delivered. Same goods, same dates, same paperwork — and no second way of splitting to build and maintain.

Split at delivery only — one level
PO line · 100 · RDD 2026-03-10
ASN · 50 · ships 03-10 ASN · 50 · ships 03-24

Deliveries attach straight to the PO line. Receipts and invoices match against that line, exactly as they do today. Nothing new to build.

Split at confirmation too — two levels
PO line · 100 · RDD 2026-03-10
Schedule 1 · 50 · confirmed 03-10
ASN · 25 · ships 03-10 ASN · 25 · ships 03-13
Schedule 2 · 50 · confirmed 03-24
ASN · 50 · ships 03-24

Each schedule can be split again by delivery. Every delivery, receipt and invoice line then has to say which schedule it belongs to, and the outstanding quantity is counted per schedule instead of per line.

The one thing that is actually lost

If the supplier cannot split the confirmation, the date for the second 50 is nowhere in the system until they ship it. The requester still sees all 100 due on 03-10, and finds out only when half of it fails to arrive. A split confirmation would have shown 03-24 from the start.

So the trade-off is simple: knowing the later date sooner, against building a second splitting level into PO, delivery, receipt and invoice. Is agreeing the plan with the supplier by phone enough?

04

If the split is not allowed

What the supplier does instead when the date cannot be met, and what it costs.

OptionHow it worksWhy it might be rightWhat it costs us
A. Supplier splitsschedule lines on the confirmation The supplier confirms 50 / 50 on two dates. The PO carries two schedule lines and both deliveries run against the same PO. Reflects what the supplier can actually do. No rework, no new PO numbers. The supplier changes a date the requester agreed to, with no approval step on our side. Confirmed stops meaning "confirmed as ordered".
B. Reject and re-issuestarting position The supplier can only confirm the line as ordered, or reject it. On a rejection Purchasing agrees a plan and issues two POs — 50 and 50, each with its own date.
  • Original PO is cancelled or superseded
  • Each new PO confirms on its own
 Every delivery date in the system was agreed by Purchasing. One PO means one quantity on one date, which keeps receipt and invoice matching simple. Rework on every date slip — a new PO, a new approval, new numbers. Slower, and the trail spreads across documents.
C. Change needs our approvalmiddle ground The supplier proposes the change — quantity, date or split. It does not take effect until a buyer approves it; until then the PO stays Open. Keeps the supplier's real capability visible without letting them change the order on their own. Needs a pending-change state, a buyer queue, and a rule for what happens if nobody acts on it. Only possible if Ariba can hold a response pending — questions 3 and 5.
05

What changes downstream

If splitting is accepted in any form, these four things have to follow it.

PO status What does Confirmed mean?

If a split is allowed, a PO can be half-confirmed. Either we add a Partially Confirmed status, or Confirmed stops guaranteeing the ordered date.

Delivery & receipt Which schedule did this GR fill?

Two schedules against one line means every delivery note and receipt has to say which schedule it belongs to, or the open quantity per date cannot be tracked.

Invoice matching Three-way match unit

Matching runs per PO line today. Split schedules mean matching a partial invoice against a partial receipt against a partial schedule.

What the requester sees One status per item

A single end-user status per item still has to be produced. With two schedules at different stages, the rule is the least-advanced one — a requester waiting on the second half sees the whole line as pending.

06

Edge cases we still have to answer

These have no obvious right answer, so they take a comment rather than a Yes/No.

OPEN QUESTION

A split confirmation arrives anyway — what should the system do?

If the supplier sends a 50 / 50 confirmation even though our rule says they may not, the message still reaches us. The options are not equally safe:

  • Reject it automatically and leave the PO Open — the supplier may not notice
  • Hold it for a buyer to look at, and keep the PO Open meanwhile
  • Accept it and flag the PO for follow-up
OPEN QUESTION

Goods are already on the way for part of the quantity

The supplier ships 50 first and registers a delivery note before any of this is settled. Rejecting the PO at that point is no longer clean — something has already physically moved.

  • Does an existing delivery note lock the PO against being rejected or split?
  • If the PO must still be split, what happens to the shipment already in transit?
