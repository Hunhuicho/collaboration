PO Confirmation — Split Schedules

A PO goes out for 100 with one requested delivery date. The supplier confirms it as 50 + 50 on two different dates. Should that be allowed at all — or should the split be agreed first and issued as two POs? Please answer Yes / No on the 8 items below and add a comment.

01

The case

One order line, one requested date — and a confirmation that comes back in two pieces.

PO issued 100 EA Item 00010 RDD 2026-03-10 Status Open
→
Supplier confirmation · schedule 1 50 EA Confirmed date 2026-03-10
Supplier confirmation · schedule 2 50 EA Confirmed date 2026-03-24
The requested date was set for the whole 100. In this flow the supplier decides on its own to break the quantity apart and move half of it two weeks out. The requester agreed to one delivery, and now there are two — with no one on our side having approved the change.
02

Partial delivery already covers this

A supplier who can only ship half now can already do exactly that — at delivery, not at confirmation.

THE ARGUMENTNothing is lost by refusing the split

Purchasing and the supplier agree the plan between themselves. The supplier confirms 100, then ships 50 and registers a delivery note, and 50 more later. The PO goes Partially Delivered, then Fully Delivered. The goods, the dates and the paperwork all end up the same — without a second splitting mechanism in the system.

Split at delivery only — one level
PO line · 100 · RDD 2026-03-10
ASN · 50 · ships 03-10 ASN · 50 · ships 03-24

Delivery documents hang straight off the line. A GR belongs to a PO line, an invoice matches a PO line. Nothing new to build.

Split at confirmation too — two levels
PO line · 100 · RDD 2026-03-10
Schedule 1 · 50 · confirmed 03-10
ASN · 25 · ships 03-10 ASN · 25 · ships 03-13
Schedule 2 · 50 · confirmed 03-24
ASN · 50 · ships 03-24

A confirmation schedule can itself be split further by delivery. Every DN, GR and invoice line now has to name which schedule it belongs to, and open quantity has to be tracked per schedule rather than per line.

The one thing that is actually lost

Without a split confirmation, the second half has no date in the system until the supplier registers its delivery note. The requester sees 100 promised for 03-10 and only learns half is late when it fails to arrive. With schedules, the later date is visible the moment the supplier confirms.

So the question is worth being blunt about: is that forward visibility worth a second splitting level across PO, delivery, receipt and invoice — or is a phone call and an agreed plan enough?

03

Three ways this could work

Option B is the working proposal. A and C are here so the trade-off is visible, not to pad the list.

OptionHow it worksWhy it might be rightWhat it costs us
A. Supplier splitsschedule lines on the confirmation The supplier confirms 50 / 50 on two dates. The PO carries two schedule lines and both deliveries run against the same PO. Reflects what the supplier can actually do. No rework, no new PO numbers. This is how most supplier networks behave by default. The supplier changes a date the requester agreed to, with no approval step on our side. Confirmed status stops meaning "confirmed as ordered".
B. Reject and re-issueworking proposal The supplier can only confirm the line as ordered, or reject it. On a rejection Purchasing agrees a plan with the supplier and issues two POs — 50 and 50, each with its own date.
  • Original PO is cancelled or superseded
  • Each new PO confirms on its own
 Every delivery date on the system was agreed by Purchasing. One PO always means one quantity on one date, which keeps GR and invoice matching simple. Rework on every date slip — a new PO, a new approval, new numbers. Slower, and the audit trail spreads across documents.
C. Split needs our approvalmiddle ground The supplier may propose a split. It does not take effect until a buyer accepts it; until then the PO stays Open and the proposal sits as a pending change. Keeps the supplier's real capability visible without letting them change the order unilaterally. Needs a pending-change state, a buyer queue and a rule for what happens if nobody acts on it.
Worth knowing before deciding

Splitting a confirmation across schedule lines is standard behaviour in supplier networks and ERP order acknowledgements — a supplier confirming part of a quantity on a later date is an ordinary message, not an error. Choosing option B means we deliberately refuse a message the interface can send us, so we need to decide what the system does when one arrives anyway.

To verify with the interface team: whether an incoming confirmation can be rejected at the message level, or whether it always lands and we handle it afterwards.

04

What changes downstream

Whichever option is chosen, these four things have to follow it.

PO status What does Confirmed mean?

If a split is allowed, a PO can be half-confirmed. Either we add a Partially Confirmed status, or Confirmed stops guaranteeing the ordered date.

Delivery & receipt Which schedule did this GR fill?

Two schedules against one line means every DN and GR has to say which schedule it belongs to, or the open quantity per date cannot be tracked.

Invoice matching Three-way match unit

Matching runs per PO line today. Split schedules mean matching a partial invoice against a partial receipt against a partial schedule.

What the requester sees One status per item

A single end-user status per item still has to be produced. With two schedules at different stages, the rule is the least-advanced one — a requester waiting on the second half sees the whole line as pending.

05

Edge cases we still have to answer

These have no obvious right answer, so they take a comment rather than a Yes/No.

OPEN QUESTION

A split confirmation arrives anyway — what should the system do?

If the supplier sends a 50 / 50 confirmation even though the rule says they may not, the message still reaches us. The options are not equally safe:

  • Reject it automatically and leave the PO Open — the supplier may not notice
  • Hold it for a buyer to look at, and keep the PO Open meanwhile
  • Accept it and flag the PO for follow-up
OPEN QUESTION

Goods are already on the way for part of the quantity

The supplier ships 50 first and registers a delivery note before any of this is settled. Rejecting the PO at that point is no longer clean — something has already physically moved.

  • Does an existing delivery note lock the PO against being rejected or split?
  • If the PO must still be split, what happens to the shipment already in transit?
